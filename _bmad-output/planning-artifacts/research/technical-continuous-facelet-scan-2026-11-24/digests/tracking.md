# Mapping moving cube views to 54 sticker positions

Research date 2026-11-24. The strongest measured multi-view result used depth and deliberate changes of viewpoint. A handheld RGB implementation reports encouraging results on a small set of recordings. Together they support testing a geometry-based scanner that can stop collecting evidence during a regrip and register the cube again when two neighboring centers are visible. [ETH Zürich paper, sections IV and VI-A](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24610v1) · [Handheld implementation and fixture notes](https://github.com/ductm54/rubik_solver/blob/74b66ff2182d101805f38bca18dcc5276c3cb90c/README.md#notes)

## Source findings

- **Direct multi-view measurement.** Amiet et al. (preprint, 17 October 2026) mapped observed stickers from different viewpoints on a 40 cm cube. Its shape has 24 proper rotational symmetries. Their system required two readable adjacent centers consistent with a known color layout. With three centers it also checked handedness. Shared edge directions determined each grid's quarter-turn orientation. A wrong but compatible center could still misassign a face. The robot recovered the exact 54-sticker net on five of five indoor sequences and three of five outdoor sequences. The two incomplete captures lacked a usable sixth view. The apparatus used RGB-D, a robot-controlled scan and 1.3 seconds of processing per frame. [ETH Zürich paper, sections III, IV and VI-A](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24610v1)
- **Handheld RGB alternative.** A browser scanner documented on 19 November 2026 captures six faces in arbitrary order. It samples a strip of the neighboring face where available and uses physical cube constraints to test possible grid rotations. Its authors report five phone recordings, two featuring nearly solved cubes. They describe an early failure where a stationary-corners rule captured only two faces even though all six had stable color reads. This supports testing stability in the moving face's coordinate system. The implementation assembles captured faces and offers manual correction. Its small, developer-maintained fixture set gives no population-wide error estimate. [Pinned implementation, design and test notes](https://github.com/ductm54/rubik_solver/blob/74b66ff2182d101805f38bca18dcc5276c3cb90c/README.md#notes)
- **Portable geometry.** Kasprzak, Szynkiewicz and Czajka (2006) grouped cell contours into up to three faces using adjacency and edge directions. Their robot-oriented study measured surface detection from one image. OpenCV 4.13 provides calibrated 3D-to-2D pose fitting, robust estimation with outliers, and refinement. Collins and Bartoli (2014) show why a small or distant planar square can have two plausible poses with similar reprojection error. Four possible in-plane grid rotations remain a separate correspondence issue even with a good corner fit. [Single-view cube paper](https://www.ia.pw.edu.pl/~wkasprza/PAP/ICCVG06.pdf) · [OpenCV pose functions](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d5/d1f/calib3d_solvePnP.html) · [Planar pose ambiguity, authors' implementation](https://github.com/tobycollins/IPPE#overview)
- **Cross-frame correspondence.** OpenCV's optical-flow tutorial explains how tracking can jump from a disappearing point to a similar point. Its sample redetects corners and checks motion backwards. CoTracker3 (Karaev et al., first published 15 October 2024) offers online and offline RGB tracking of specified points. Its offline model can use later observations to interpolate through an occlusion. Published benchmarks assess point position and visibility. Sticker identity, color and 54-slot accuracy need independent verification. The primary repository lists CC BY-NC for the majority of its code. [OpenCV optical flow](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d4/dee/tutorial_optical_flow.html) · [CoTracker3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11831) · [Authors' implementation and licensing](https://github.com/facebookresearch/co-tracker#license)

## Experiment implied by the sources

1. **Portable baseline.** Fit a projective 3×3 lattice to visible cell boundaries. Initialize the named cube axes from adjacent centers and their shared edge. Use calibrated, noncoplanar correspondences when two or three faces are visible. A single isolated face can contribute tentative local observations. Preserve alternate rotations until an overlapping keyframe or new adjacent center distinguishes them. At short intervals, track textured lattice corners with forward-backward checks and periodically refit the grid from image data. Freeze evidence accumulation during loss of support. [Planar pose ambiguity](https://github.com/tobycollins/IPPE#overview) · [Multi-view assembly](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24610v1) · [OpenCV optical flow](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d4/dee/tutorial_optical_flow.html)
2. **Higher-compute RGB comparison.** On identical saved clips, test offline point tracking or manually marked keyframes, plus joint fitting of poses across overlapping views. Use the same named slots, visibility rules and color reader as the portable path. Allow future frames in this reference and report its offline advantage separately. A human should verify keyframe labels against the known physical state. An interpolated hidden corner may help pose estimation, while a color observation requires visible sticker pixels. [CoTracker3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11831) · [Multi-view visibility and net assembly](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24610v1)
3. **Recovery and scoring.** Retain the frame, sampled polygon and competing orientation for each observation. Reacquire after a hand covers the cube using two adjacent reliable centers or an overlap that uniquely fixes the orientation. A layer twist changes the physical state mid-scan. Flag suspected nonrigid motion and reacquire a consistent scan before combining conflicting readings. A twist hidden entirely behind a hand can be unobservable until affected stickers reappear. Measure exact 54-slot agreement, wrong completions, incomplete scans, and correct re-registration after regrips against independently recorded truth. Count a missing or persistently occluded sticker as unresolved. [ETH Zürich scan experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24610v1) · [OpenCV tracking failure example](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d4/dee/tutorial_optical_flow.html)
