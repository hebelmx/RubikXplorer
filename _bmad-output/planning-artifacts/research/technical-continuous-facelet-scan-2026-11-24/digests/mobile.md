# Mobile detectors for visible facelets

Research checked 24 November 2026. Prove the continuous mapping algorithm on PC first (verified keyframes + a clean geometry detector), then target Cubentia's already-bundled LiteRT runtime with an extended `render_cube.py` dataset rather than adding `ncnn` or starting with NanoDet. Public benchmarks leave sticker recall, sustained camera performance and installed size on the target devices unresolved.

## What to train and export

- **Cubentia-owned LiteRT path (recommended first mobile target).** Cubentia already bundles `com.google.ai.edge.litert:litert:2.3.2` (0 additional native `.so` bytes, passes R8 and 16 KB ELF alignment) and owns a seeded, domain-randomized 3D renderer (`tools/corner-model/render_cube.py`) with real Lenovo/S24 FE palettes, hands, glare, YUV chroma subsampling, and dark glossy reflections. **Required renderer extension before training:** `render_cube.py` is currently a centered 7-keypoint 3-face corner-only renderer that labels `flat_2d_face` as a hard negative (`L604–L621`) and paints hands after warping (`L560–L561`) with no occlusion mask. Before training a continuous detector, extend it to (a) full 8-vertex SE(3) projection with backface culling for **1, 2, and 3 visible faces**, (b) arbitrary off-center full-frame placement, (c) per-sticker hand-occlusion visibility masks, and (d) treating `flat_2d_face` as a 1-face positive (otherwise the trained detector will reject near-frontal views). Freeze the **union** of `tools/sticker-model/holdout.json['heldout']` and `Fixtures/corner/groups.json[split=='heldout']` out of `render_cube.py`'s live-crop loader and all detector selection. Two compact LiteRT-native designs fit this stack and run identically on PC and Android:
  1. **Free-pose multi-face quad/keypoint detector:** Locate 1–3 visible face quads anywhere in the frame. Each quad immediately yields an ordered 3×3 projective grid (avoiding 27-box NMS clutter) and feeds both registration and unwarped CIELAB color reading (note: Story 10.23's `sticker_reader.tflite` failed its safety bar at `tau = 0.99` with 4 confidently wrong reads — 3.7% on Lenovo backlit — so treat it as a shadow comparator only unless retrained or gated by multi-frame/CIELAB agreement, and count its 108 KB asset + `3×` wrapper classes if ever promoted out of `debug/sticker/`).
  2. **Geometry-only sticker-center heatmap + offset (CenterNet-style):** Train a narrow MobileNet/inverted-residual backbone outputting a high-resolution heatmap for visible sticker centers (plus optional center-sticker class and subcell offsets) on the extended renderer + non-held-out `Fixtures/corner` & `Fixtures/live`. Preserve an observation mask through occlusion and leave cube-relative rotation to multiview registration. [Objects as Points, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07850) · [Keras MobileNet backbones](https://keras.io/api/applications/mobilenet/) · [Google keypoint model and measurements](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_detector) · [versioned reference weights](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_detector/blaze_face_short_range/float16/1/blaze_face_short_range.tflite)
- **Box baseline (PC comparator / fallback).** NanoDet-Plus-m is an Apache-2.0 anchor-free detector with a 1.17-million-parameter model. Its authors document training on custom box annotations in COCO, Pascal VOC or YOLO format, exporting PyTorch to ONNX and converting that export to ncnn. Train one class for a visible sticker and return a box, center and score for each observation. Try 320 and 416 pixels on the same clips. The authors report COCO small-object AP of 0.083 at 320, making narrowly projected side stickers an important independent test. Because adding an ncnn native library is tightly constrained by NFR-11 (see Size accounting below), treat NanoDet as a PC accuracy comparator unless a stripped CPU-only runtime or LiteRT export proves within budget. [NanoDet training and benchmarks](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet) · [desktop/export pipeline](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet/tree/main/demo_ncnn) · [320-pixel configuration and small-object result](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet/blob/main/config/nanodet-plus-m_320.yml)
- **PC to Android.** Train and evaluate the heatmap in float on PC, calibrate full integer quantization on representative training-side cube clips, convert with integer built-in ops, then replay held-out clips through the exported file on PC. Bundle that same file in the Android build and compare tensors, peak locations and final assignments on fixed frames. Record RGB byte order, crop/letterboxing, rotation, input range, quantization scales and the inverse image transform. Compare candidates after postprocessing and allow enough outputs for three visible faces (up to 27 stickers). NanoDet's alternative ncnn route has a documented offline calibration and conversion pipeline. [LiteRT integer conversion](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert/conversion/tensorflow/quantization/post_training_integer_quant) · [Android local inference](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert/android) · [ncnn calibration and INT8 export](https://github.com/Tencent/ncnn/wiki/quantized-int8-inference)
- **Other controls.** EfficientDet-Lite0 provides a documented custom box-training and quantized LiteRT export route, but its stock asset exceeds the spare space below. Ultralytics' pose route supports a fixed number of custom keypoints with visibility and LiteRT export. Its publisher offers AGPL-3.0 and enterprise licensing. Treat it as a PC comparator until the intended distribution and an actual custom binary have been evaluated. [EfficientDet transfer learning](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert/libraries/modify/object_detection) · [custom pose labels](https://docs.ultralytics.com/datasets/pose/) · [export formats](https://docs.ultralytics.com/modes/export/) · [publisher licensing](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)

## Size accounting

Story 10.18's historical `checkCameraSize` projection was **26.86 MB** (using a 2026-11-21 release baseline of **12.41 MB** and a 406,100-byte corner model) against the **30 MB** ceiling (NFR-11). **That headroom figure is now stale:** by Story 10.23 (`docs/evidence/10-23/release-size.md`, 2026-11-22), `checkReleaseSize` had already grown to **12.98 MB (`12,980,683` B, +575 KB)**, and `CameraSizePolicy.kt` charges **`3 × classBytes`** for every camera class added since Story 10.18 (Stories 10.21, #313, #321, #323, #325). That shifts the realistic current camera-enabled projection to **~27.4–28.0 MB**, leaving roughly **~2.0–2.5 MB** of actual headroom (or ~2.4–2.9 MB if replacing the 406 KB corner model) before any new runtime or Kotlin code. Additionally, `CameraSizePolicy.kt` explicitly **forbids `opencv` and `tensorflow` artifacts** on Android.

The table below keeps the Story 10.18 baseline (`26.86 MB`) beside the Story 10.23 release-delta floor (`≥ 27.44 MB`, before post-10.18 camera class growth) as arithmetic projections before any added runtime or packaging overhead. Downloaded reference weights have generic labels; a custom training run produces a new artifact.

I inspected the ZIP directory of NanoDet's 26 December 2021 release (2,218,050 bytes compressed), and counted the bytes of Google's versioned EfficientDet model. On the adjusted floor, the uncompressed NanoDet FP32 pair leaves essentially **zero room (~0.14 MB)** for any added runtime or camera classes if both models remain. The separate ncnn Android demo released on 20 October 2026 carries a 12,624,208-byte arm64 native library (4,704,373 bytes compressed) linking a Vulkan build of ncnn, OpenCV and camera/demo code, which exceeds the allowance. Note that `CheckCameraSizeTask.kt` currently scans only `androidx.camera` and `com.google.ai.edge.litert` dependency groups (ignoring any unmonitored `jniLibs/*.so`), so any non-LiteRT runtime must be measured in a minified `arm64-v8a` APK if ever considered for Android. ## Which speed measurements transfer

Cubentia's own Story 10.18 device benchmarks on the **Lenovo TB128XU** (`docs/evidence/10-18/litert-model-evaluation.md`) show `corner_keypoints.tflite` (`128×128`, 198K params, LiteRT XNNPACK 4 threads) at **13.96–14.09 ms median** in debug (gate D1) and **6.35–6.46 ms median** under R8 minification (gate D3). That R8 measurement doubles the realistic LiteRT inference headroom on the budget tablet compared with the debug figure alone, though a multi-face or higher-resolution continuous scanner will still cost more than a 128×128 guided crop.

NanoDet's authors report 11.97 ms at 320 and 19.77 ms at 416 on four Cortex-A76 cores of a Kirin 980 using ncnn. The table gives one latency per resolution and does not attribute it to INT8. Google reports 29.31 ms mean CPU task latency on Pixel 6 for EfficientDet-Lite0 INT8. Qualcomm's archived model card reports estimated 6.50 ms and 4.84 ms for two Galaxy S24 Android 14 NPU profiles of a 640 × 640 YOLOv8 detector. The example does not identify the runtime for each timing. Samsung specifies an Exynos 2400e in the S24 FE. These are individual measurements of different workloads and hardware. [NanoDet measurements](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet#benchmarks) · [Google task benchmark](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/object_detector) · [pinned Qualcomm model card](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/YOLOv8-Detection/raw/cf931cdf8ce74f5732dd29c23babce10c0057311/README.md) · [Samsung hardware report](https://images.samsung.com/is/content/samsung/assets/global/ir/docs/2024_4Q_Interim_Report.pdf)
