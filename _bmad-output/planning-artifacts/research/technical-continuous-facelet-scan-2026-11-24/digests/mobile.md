# Mobile detectors for visible facelets

Research checked 24 November 2026. Start the box experiment on a PC with NanoDet-Plus-m and compare a small center-heatmap model that exports directly to LiteRT. The weights of a compact detector can fit the supplied app-size projection. Public benchmarks leave sticker recall, sustained camera performance and installed size on the target devices unresolved.

## What to train and export

- **Box baseline.** NanoDet-Plus-m is an Apache-2.0 anchor-free detector with a 1.17-million-parameter model. Its authors document training on custom box annotations in COCO, Pascal VOC or YOLO format, exporting PyTorch to ONNX and converting that export to ncnn. Their desktop demo reads recorded videos and webcams and shares the ncnn model format with Android. Train one class for a visible sticker and return a box, center and score for each observation. Try 320 and 416 pixels on the same clips. The authors report COCO small-object AP of 0.083 at 320, making narrowly projected side stickers an important independent test. Angle the cube, partly cover a face, include distractors and record recall by apparent sticker size. [NanoDet training and benchmarks](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet) · [desktop/export pipeline](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet/tree/main/demo_ncnn) · [320-pixel configuration and small-object result](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet/blob/main/config/nanodet-plus-m_320.yml)
- **Center or corner candidate for the existing runtime.** Experiment with a narrow MobileNet backbone and a high-resolution heatmap for visible sticker centers, with optional subcell offsets. Alternatively predict a quadrilateral per visible face and test its nine projected centers against local image evidence. A square face presents four equivalent starting corners before orientation is resolved. Use cyclically equivalent labels or a documented image-space convention and leave the cube-relative rotation to multiview registration. Preserve an observation mask through occlusion. CenterNet establishes the heatmap/offset method. Google's 229,746-byte short-range BlazeFace asset demonstrates a box-and-keypoint network at a mobile scale for **human faces**. Neither source establishes cube accuracy or the size of a custom export. [Objects as Points, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07850) · [Keras MobileNet backbones](https://keras.io/api/applications/mobilenet/) · [Google keypoint model and measurements](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_detector) · [versioned reference weights](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_detector/blaze_face_short_range/float16/1/blaze_face_short_range.tflite)
- **PC to Android.** Train and evaluate the heatmap in float on PC, calibrate full integer quantization on representative training-side cube clips, convert with integer built-in ops, then replay held-out clips through the exported file on PC. Bundle that same file in the Android build and compare tensors, peak locations and final assignments on fixed frames. Record RGB byte order, crop/letterboxing, rotation, input range, quantization scales and the inverse image transform. Compare candidates after postprocessing and allow enough outputs for three visible faces (up to 27 stickers). NanoDet's alternative ncnn route has a documented offline calibration and conversion pipeline. [LiteRT integer conversion](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert/conversion/tensorflow/quantization/post_training_integer_quant) · [Android local inference](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert/android) · [ncnn calibration and INT8 export](https://github.com/Tencent/ncnn/wiki/quantized-int8-inference)
- **Other controls.** EfficientDet-Lite0 provides a documented custom box-training and quantized LiteRT export route, but its stock asset exceeds the spare space below. Ultralytics' pose route supports a fixed number of custom keypoints with visibility and LiteRT export. Its publisher offers AGPL-3.0 and enterprise licensing. Treat it as a PC comparator until the intended distribution and an actual custom binary have been evaluated. [EfficientDet transfer learning](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert/libraries/modify/object_detection) · [custom pose labels](https://docs.ultralytics.com/datasets/pose/) · [export formats](https://docs.ultralytics.com/modes/export/) · [publisher licensing](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)

## Size accounting

The supplied planning figures are approximately 26.86 MB installed, including a 406,100-byte prior model, against a 30 MB ceiling. Assuming decimal MB and a one-for-one raw-byte change, the allowance is about 3.14 MB if both models remain, or 3.5461 MB total for replacement weights and incremental runtime if replacement preserves all required functions. The figures below are arithmetic projections before any runtime, packaging, alignment, or device-specific differences. Downloaded reference weights have generic labels. A custom training run produces a new artifact.

| Published asset | Model bytes | Projected with both models | Projected with replacement |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NanoDet-Plus-m, 416 px, published ncnn pair | 2,397,184 binary weights + 23,123 graph = **2,420,307** | 29.280 MB | 28.874 MB |
| NanoDet-Plus-m, INT8 | **~1.2 MB**, author's rounded model-size claim | ~28.06 MB | ~27.65 MB |
| EfficientDet-Lite0 INT8, 320 px | **4,602,795** | 31.463 MB | 31.057 MB |

I inspected the ZIP directory of NanoDet's 26 December 2021 release (2,218,050 bytes compressed), and counted the bytes of Google's versioned EfficientDet model. The uncompressed NanoDet pair leaves only about 0.720 MB for an added runtime if both models remain. The separate ncnn Android demo released on 20 October 2026 carries a 12,624,208-byte arm64 native library (4,704,373 bytes compressed) linking a Vulkan build of ncnn, OpenCV and camera/demo code. That binary exceeds the allowance. The cost of a minimal CPU-only build or a validated export into an already bundled runtime remains unmeasured. Compare camera-enabled builds with the same ABI, delivery and install procedure, and report installed bytes separately from APK and model bytes. ## Which speed measurements transfer

NanoDet's authors report 11.97 ms at 320 and 19.77 ms at 416 on four Cortex-A76 cores of a Kirin 980 using ncnn. The table gives one latency per resolution and does not attribute it to INT8. Google reports 29.31 ms mean CPU task latency on Pixel 6 for EfficientDet-Lite0 INT8. Qualcomm's archived model card reports estimated 6.50 ms and 4.84 ms for two Galaxy S24 Android 14 NPU profiles of a 640 × 640 YOLOv8 detector. The example does not identify the runtime for each timing. Samsung specifies an Exynos 2400e in the S24 FE. These are individual measurements of different workloads and hardware. [NanoDet measurements](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet#benchmarks) · [Google task benchmark](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/object_detector) · [pinned Qualcomm model card](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/YOLOv8-Detection/raw/cf931cdf8ce74f5732dd29c23babce10c0057311/README.md) · [Samsung hardware report](https://images.samsung.com/is/content/samsung/assets/global/ir/docs/2024_4Q_Interim_Report.pdf)

Geekbench 6 publishes a common CPU workload across the three target models. It runs MobileNet v1 SSD on pre-sized 300 × 300 photos, 16 images in single-core mode and 64 in multicore mode. The following user-submitted run records show device variability. Suite versions and OS builds vary. Batch throughput omits camera capture and carries no information about per-frame tail latency, thermal stability, export precision or cube accuracy. No primary benchmark was located for the **same trained cube detector** on these three devices. Start physical-device profiling with the Lenovo. Measure initialization, warm steady state and sustained camera-to-overlay p50/p95 by stage, processed/dropped frames, sticker misses, incorrect commitments, and time to a correct six-face state. Time detector-only and end-to-end runs separately, under the same capture, lighting, thermal and thread settings.
