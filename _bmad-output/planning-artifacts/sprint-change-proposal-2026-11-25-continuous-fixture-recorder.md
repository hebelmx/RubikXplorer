# RubikXplorer sprint change proposal — record and replay continuous Quick Scan fixtures

**25 November 2026 · Approved by the product owner on 25 November 2026 · Planning only; stories scheduled after the dependency gate**

Keep the ability to record a failing scan and replay it offline when more evidence is needed. After the continuous Quick Scan pipeline is finished, migrate the developer recorder and its evaluation workflow to the same pipeline used by the app. Retire old two-corner code only where that migration removes its last consumer, and never the capture that Case recognition still needs.

## 1. Trigger and evidence

Story 10.23 (shadow sticker-reader comparison) used two distinct tools. The debug Android fixture recorder captures labelled examples. The offline Gradle task `exportKotlinStickerReads` reads those examples and calls `QuickScanClassification.report` and `.read` to produce the old baseline. Story 10.31 (continuous detector integration) already retired the shadow model and its Android hook. The capability to carry forward is capture and replay. [Recorder entry point](../../app/src/camera/debug/AndroidManifest.xml) · [Offline exporter](../../core/domain/src/evaluation/kotlin/dev/cubentia/domain/recognition/evaluation/sticker/KotlinStickerReads.kt) · [Story 10.23 evidence inventory](../../docs/evidence/10-23/inventory.md)

The recorder saves two prescribed holds per face. Its adb-triggered burst (`RecorderBurst`, Quick Scan reliability spike 2026-11-22) writes consecutive frames to `frames.jsonl` with a JPEG every Nth frame, but each line is a two-corner reading and a `SideLight` check, not a continuous-detector observation. Story 10.32 retires the only replay of those bursts (`QuickScanBurstReplayTest`, `RecordedBursts`). Neither path runs the continuous detector or mapper, so neither can reproduce the order in which faces appeared, a regrip or a conflict developing across frames. Story A reuses the burst's adb trigger only. The burst buffers every line and writes once at the end, which does not fit image capture.

Story 10.32's acceptance in `epics.md` listed `QuickScanClassification.kt`, `CornerHold.kt` and `SideLight.kt` for removal. Its spec's PO scope (Q7) keeps `QuickScanClassification`, `exportKotlinStickerReads`, `evaluateQuickScan` and the recorder's `SideLight` use, trims `CornerHold` to Case capture and the recorder, and records the retired burst replay. **Change applied with this approval:** the Story 10.32 cleanup criterion in `epics.md` now names that scope, so a later pass against the epic cannot delete the recorder's or exporter's dependencies. [Sprint record](../implementation-artifacts/sprint-status.yaml) · [Approved pipeline sequence](sprint-change-proposal-2026-11-24-continuous-quick-scan.md)

The change belongs in Epic 10 (camera recognition). Architecture decision AD-9 (camera and frame handling) permits a debug-only fixture writer and prohibits persistence of frames in release and benchmark builds; its current debug exception describes a downscaled frame per face. Story A therefore needs an AD-9 amendment, drafted with the story and approved before implementation. It allows an operator to retain a bounded sequence of cube frames in local debug storage, and to commit curated sequences to `Fixtures/`. The writer belongs solely to the developer recorder. The PRD's privacy note ("camera frames are never stored or sent") describes learner builds; the amendment adds one clarifying sentence there that developer debug builds on operator-owned devices are excepted, so the promise and the practice stay aligned.

AD-9 also keeps raw frames and Android types out of `:core:domain`, and LiteRT runs only as an Android library (it cannot execute a `.tflite` in a JVM source set). Replay therefore splits in two:
- **State replay** — recorded observations through a fresh mapper — runs in the `:core:domain` evaluation source set.
- **Image replay** — saved detector inputs through the LiteRT detector — runs as an instrumented debug test on an emulator or device.

## 3. Recommended approach

Schedule two deferred stories in Epic 10: **A**, continuous capture and offline replay (medium effort); **B**, retirement of verified dead Quick Scan two-corner code (small), which starts only after A's capture and replay demonstrations pass on a physical device. Splitting keeps a slow caller inventory from holding back the diagnostic tool. Replay fidelity, frame-capture cost on the analyzer thread and shared legacy callers are the main risks. Reverting pipeline work would reintroduce the fixed holds. Revising the product requirements would add scope without addressing either tool. Fixture collection beyond the examples needed to verify the migration is driven by a specific evidence gap.

## 4. Proposed backlog change

**Today:** the completed recorder, label checks and shadow-reader evaluation describe their original fixture formats. There is no post-pipeline story tying continuous capture, offline replay and retirement together.

**Additions after the dependency gate:** Story A, *Record and replay a continuous Quick Scan session in the developer fixture recorder*, takes the first five rows below. Story B, *Retire the Quick-Scan-only two-corner recorder paths*, takes the last row and depends on A. Scheduled as Story 10.34 (A) and Story 10.35 (B) in `epics.md`. As a developer investigating a Quick Scan failure, I can deliberately record a scan while turning the cube, preserve the order of observations, and replay it against independently verified labels to reproduce where the result diverged.

| Acceptance | Demonstration |
| --- | --- |
| Use the app’s finished pipeline | Launch the existing debug recorder via its activity, start a session, turn the cube naturally, then stop or discard. Feed the same model, lattice fitter, sampler and stateful mapper used by the Solve-tab scan. Preserve Guided single-face capture and the corner-mode capture Case recognition uses. In continuous mode the corner detector is not initialised (checked by a debug test). Capture copies data off the analyzer thread to a writer executor; record analyzer latency with and without capture, and mark a session whose capture dropped extra frames as defective. |
| Produce a usable local fixture | A versioned group records the session ID, device, cube, lighting, model identity (file SHA-256), LiteRT version and delegate/thread configuration, build commit, frame order and timing, rotation/crop geometry, and sampled observations needed by the replay stage. For an image fixture, the story spec chooses what is stored (the detector's model input, or a downscaled source frame with its transform) and which model changes each supports. Record any sampling or dropped frames. The spec fixes the frame cap, stored resolution, per-session size and memory budget. Writes stream in chunks and finish atomically, so an interrupted write cannot be mistaken for a complete group. Preserve loss of tracking, empty observations and partial or failed sessions. Save to app-specific local storage only on operator request. Only operator-curated groups enter `Fixtures/` (343 MB today, no LFS), under a size limit a CI check enforces or an LFS/external-storage decision. The capture hook is a debug-only seam (an optional binding provided from the debug camera source set), so the shipped continuous code holds no writer; a source-set test asserts no frame-writer classes in release or benchmark builds. |
| Keep truth independent | Store operator-confirmed reference stickers separately from predicted colors, with unknowns explicit. Apply the Story 10.28 rules (nine of each color, review provenance, explicit override reason) through a format-neutral 54-sticker check in `:core:domain`, shared with the two-hold format, so Story B cannot remove it. A partial or deliberately invalid example stays available for diagnosis with an explicit outcome. Keep a capture session and its derivatives together in the chosen tuning or held-out partition. |
| Replay offline | Supply a documented replacement for the continuous use case of the old exporter. Replay ordered observations with a fresh mapper per session. State replay must be exact for the mapper's snapshots and cue stream; screen-level timing (the completion hold, hand-over choices) is outside state replay. For a known complete example, reproduce the recorded net in canonical face order and compare against operator truth. For a partial or failed example, reproduce its last confirmed net and cue or conflict without filling unknowns. For an image fixture, the instrumented replay runs the saved inputs through the recorded detector version and checks the detections against the captured baseline within a tolerance set in the story spec, then runs the same fixture with a candidate detector. Report the replay starting stage, observed coverage and label mismatches. |
| Preserve historical evidence | Existing fixtures remain readable in their original formats, and each fixture root keeps its reader: `Fixtures/live` (`evaluateScanAccuracy`, the live baseline, `evaluateRecognitionApproaches`, `LiveFixture` decoding), `Fixtures/corner` with `tools/sticker-model/holdout.json` (`exportKotlinStickerReads`), `Fixtures/matrix` (`evaluateMatrix`), and `Fixtures/labeled` and the photo groups. Document the archived revision and command for each legacy score, including the burst replay retired in Story 10.32 (`docs/evidence/10-32/camera-size.md`). Verify at least one old fixture and one new continuous group replay. |
| Retire only proven dead code (Story B) | On the final merged pipeline, inventory Android, domain, evaluation, Gradle, fixture-decoder and test callers. Move each remaining consumer to its replacement or document its continuing owner. Retire a legacy command only after Story A's demonstrations pass and its archived revision and command are documented. Corner-mode capture stays while `CASE_RECOGNITION` exists. Verify Case recognition, Guided capture and old fixture decoding still pass. |

**Cleanup candidates to verify at execution** (against the tree after Story 10.32). The recorder uses `QuickScanPlan` and `CenterCheck` (moved to `CornerCapturePlan.kt` by Story 10.32), `SideLight` and two-corner capture helpers, and `RecorderBurst` uses `SideLight`. `exportKotlinStickerReads` uses `QuickScanClassification`. Case capture uses `CornerHold`; the camera adapter also has shared `CornerLocator`, `CornerReader` and `CornerGuide` consumers. **Not candidates:** `evaluateQuickScan` and `ScanDiagnostics.onQuickScanRun` measure the continuous scan for Story 10.33. Any change to release `ScanDiagnostics` or `app/src/main` callers is product code and needs product-code review, not tooling review. A disabled release flag is insufficient evidence of dead code.
