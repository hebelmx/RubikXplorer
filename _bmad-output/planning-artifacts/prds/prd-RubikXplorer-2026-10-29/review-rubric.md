# PRD Quality Review — RubikXplorer MVP

## Overall verdict
This PRD has a real thesis ("a **tutor with a solver inside it**", §1), an honesty principle that runs from the Glossary through FR-9/FR-10/FR-23, unusually strong testable consequences for the cube engine, Course and Library (FR-4, FR-14, FR-15, FR-18), and a named cut order. It is close to launch-grade. The risks are concentrated in four places. Two journeys contradict the FRs they are meant to realize: UJ-1 contradicts FR-9's honesty rule, and UJ-5 contradicts FR-27's gating. The camera scope moved into P0 and has acceptance bounds only for the Guided 6-face Scan. The ~14-week solo schedule is still an assumption. And a few stale or mis-indexed references (OQ-5 "10 weeks", the Assumptions Index roundtrip, UJ-4's "§6") will mislead downstream extraction unless they are fixed before `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` runs.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions. Examples: Kotlin over MAUI/Unity with its reason (addendum §A), "Guided Scan is the release-blocking path" (FR-22), "no AI or LLM that invents Moves" (§6.1), and an explicit ordered de-scope list in the §6.2 [NOTE FOR PM]: "cut in this order: Quick Scan to 'beta' (FR-22) → Case recognition (FR-28) → …". That cut list is the most decision-useful paragraph in the document. Open Questions are genuinely open (OQ-3, OQ-4 and OQ-7 are real spikes).

The weaknesses are three. First, the schedule decision is unresolved yet load-bearing: the whole P0 rests on "about 14 weeks [ASSUMPTION]", and OQ-9 asks to confirm it, but nothing gives a date by which it must be decided or says what happens if the week 1–2 recognition spike fails. Second, the trade-off of pulling camera into P0 is named only as schedule cost. The PRD does not say what was given up: the developer is also learning native Android (addendum §A), and the larger scope delays the pilot that SM-1/SM-2 depend on. Third, the Index entry "§1 / Memlog — the user's 'yes' accepted the proposed defaults for algorithm count, animation approach, LL progression and phasing" records a blanket acceptance rather than individual decisions. A reader cannot tell which of those defaults were actively chosen.

### Findings
- **high** Spike-failure branch missing (§6.2 NOTE, OQ-7, addendum §F P0.1). The spike "picks the CV approach", but no outcome is defined for "no approach meets targets on 2 devices". The cut order covers only schedule slip, not technical infeasibility of the release-blocking Guided Scan, and Gate 6 makes the camera a hard production gate. *Fix:* add a spike exit criterion (e.g. a per-sticker accuracy floor on the test matrix), and a decision rule if it is missed: ship with the Manual Cube Editor only and move camera to P1, or delay release.
- **medium** OQ-9 has no decision deadline (§9). "confirm the revised P0 schedule … or which scan uses to move to P1" is the biggest scope lever and has no owner or date. *Fix:* tie it to the end of the recognition spike (week 2).
- **low** Blanket acceptance logged as an assumption (§10, last entry). "§1 / Memlog" points to an artifact that does not exist in the PRD, and it bundles four decisions. *Fix:* either state each of the four as a decision in its own section, or remove the entry.

## Substance over theater — strong
The Vision is specific to this product and could not be swapped into another cube app ("respond to mistakes with a targeted correction instead of a full reset"). NFRs carry product-specific numbers: 250 ms quarter turn, 3 s solver p95, ≥ 99.5% per-sticker accuracy, no network permission, a data-safety form that declares "no data collected". Counter-metrics SM-C1..C3 are thoughtful, and SM-C3 especially ("a Learner-set label, so it can be inflated freely") is earned. There are five personas, one over the rubric's soft limit, but each drives scope: Marco → FR-27, Lucía → FR-22/FR-25, Laura → NFR-12 and §7. That is not theater.

### Findings
- **medium** Lucía is a declared Non-User, yet she drives a P0 capture mode (§2.2 vs UJ-3, FR-22). §2.2 says people who only want a solution are "not optimized for", yet UJ-3 is built around her, and Quick Scan exists mainly for her speed. The cut order already drops Quick Scan first, which is consistent. Even so, the PRD should say plainly that UJ-3 is a secondary, conversion-funnel journey. *Fix:* label UJ-3 as secondary, or reframe Lucía as a "convertible" user and state what conversion means ("Want to learn how to do this yourself?").
- **low** UJ-4 resolution is hand-waved (UJ-4). "→ covered by §5 NFRs and §6" is a pointer, not a journey outcome, and §6 is the wrong section (see Mechanical notes).

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the beginner Course, Diagnosis and Physical-cube Mode follow from it. Scope kind is an *experience* MVP plus a portfolio deliverable, and §7's release gates fit that. SMs mostly validate the thesis: first solve, independent solve, recovery and comprehension, rather than DAU.

Coherence weakens in two places. First, of the four camera uses, only Quick Check (FR-26) serves the thesis directly by making the tutor *know* rather than guess. Start-from-my-cube (FR-27) and Case recognition (FR-28) are convenience features, and addendum §G admits competitors already have scanning. The cut order implicitly agrees, so the PRD would be more coherent if §4.8 said that Quick Check and scan-to-solve are core and the rest are opportunistic. Second, the second functional JTBD ("help me get faster by learning the real last-layer algorithms") has no success metric. SM-C3 tells you what *not* to measure, but nothing tells you whether 2-look LL works.

### Findings
- **medium** No success metric for the LL progression (§8 vs §2.1, FR-17, UJ-2). FR-17/FR-18 are P0, and the "78 Algorithms" are a marketed scope item, yet no SM validates them. *Fix:* add a pilot SM, e.g. "share of Course finishers who complete the 2-look LL mini-Course and solve an LL with 2-look Algorithms without hints" (the UJ-2 climax).
- **medium** Pilot size can't carry the percentages (§8 SM-1/2/3/8, OQ-5). Using 5–8 testers to set "share of" baselines and SM-8 scan success rates gives numbers with very wide error bars, and Gate 4 requires them to "complete the Course" over "several sessions" inside addendum §F's 2-week P0.6. *Fix:* state that pilot SMs are qualitative or directional at n≈8, and either lengthen the pilot window or start the closed test earlier (e.g. after P0.3).
- **low** SM-7 portfolio impact is untestable ("Qualitative"). *Fix:* add proxies the developer controls, e.g. repo public with README and architecture doc by release, plus a count of applications that link it.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Strong FRs: FR-1 (sticker-by-sticker equality, 250 ms), FR-4 (schema test fails the build; every Lesson has a driven path), FR-14 (named validation rules), FR-15 (randomized corpus), FR-18 (inverse-apply verification fails the build), FR-23, FR-27, FR-28 (ambiguity → list candidates). NFR-4 is exemplary.

Gaps: several FRs have no consequences at all, and the camera features outside the Guided Scan have no acceptance bounds. Two journeys also describe behavior the FRs forbid or leave unspecified. Story creation will hit these first.

### Findings
- **high** UJ-1 contradicts FR-9 (UJ-1 Path vs FR-9, FR-10). In Physical-cube Mode Ana "reports that her cube doesn't match the picture. The tutor explains that the cross was disturbed." Without a Scan or a Learner-entered Move, FR-9 requires "Certainty is *unknown* and the tutor asks for more information." The flagship journey therefore demonstrates the very behavior the PRD calls trust-breaking. *Fix:* rewrite UJ-1 so that the tutor offers a Quick Check (FR-26) or asks which Move she made, and only *then* diagnoses "cross disturbed".
- **high** UJ-5 contradicts FR-27/FR-4 on gating (UJ-5 vs FR-27 consequence 2). FR-27 says "Prerequisite gating (FR-4) still applies to *unlocking*: the scan suggests where to *practice*". UJ-5 has Marco continue straight into *First-layer corners* (Stage 3) on first launch. Either Stage 3 is locked, or placement implicitly unlocks it. That second reading relies on FR-4's skip-ahead, which is itself an [ASSUMPTION]. *Fix:* decide explicitly. For example: "placement unlocks the proposed Stage and all earlier Stages as *completed-not-mastered*." Then state that in FR-27 and FR-4.
- **high** No acceptance bounds for Quick Scan, Quick Check or Case recognition (§4.8 Feature NFRs, FR-22, FR-26, FR-28). The accuracy targets cover only the Guided 6-face Scan. Quick Scan "beta" is triggered by "falls short of the Guided 6-face Scan", which is a relative and undefined bar. Case recognition has a 1 s speed target but no correctness target, and Quick Check has none at all. All four are P0. *Fix:* add per-mode targets (even provisional [ASSUMPTION] ones). Define the beta/GA threshold in absolute terms. Add a "wrong Case named" rate for FR-28.
- **medium** Sticker Confidence calibration is unbounded (FR-21, Glossary *Scan Result*, addendum §A "only a well-calibrated 'uncertain'"). The design depends on wrong stickers being flagged *uncertain*, yet nothing bounds the rate of confidently-wrong stickers. *Fix:* add a target such as "≤ X% of misclassified stickers are marked *confident*" to the §4.8 accuracy NFR.
- **medium** FR-26's "verified" may be overclaimed from a partial Quick Check (FR-26 consequence 2 vs FR-10). A Quick Check sees only "the faces the last Moves affected". Several alternative Moves can produce the same partial observation, and FR-10 forbids "a false *verified*". *Fix:* allow *verified* only when the observed faces are consistent with exactly one candidate Move, and say so.
- **medium** FR-16, FR-25 and FR-17 have thin or no consequences. FR-16 has only an Out-of-Scope list; it does not say what the "plain-language note" says for arbitrary Kociemba output, or how Physical-cube Mode playback is confirmed. FR-25 has none. FR-17 does not say what completes or masters the mini-Course, or how 2-look Algorithms relate to Library entries and Algorithm Status. *Fix:* add 1–3 testable consequences for each. For FR-16, specify the note template (e.g. face + direction + piece-group affected).
- **medium** No FR covers UJ-1's resume behavior (UJ-1 Edge case). "on reopening … the app asks her to confirm her physical cube still matches the Expected State". FR-19 restores the Checkpoint, and FR-8 covers mode switches, not resume. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-19 or FR-8 for resuming in Physical-cube Mode.
- **medium** Per-Algorithm Mastery Condition is dangling (Glossary *Mastery Condition* "per-Stage (or per-Algorithm)" vs FR-20, SM-C3). No FR defines Algorithm mastery, and Algorithm Status is Learner-set. *Fix:* either remove "per-Algorithm" from the Glossary or define it, likely under P1 FR-29.
- **medium** UJ-6 auto-sets Algorithm Status (UJ-6 Resolution vs Glossary *Algorithm Status*). "it's added to his *Learning* list" conflicts with Algorithm Status being "the Learner's own label". *Fix:* make it an offer ("Add to Learning?"), or add the auto-set behavior to FR-28.
- **medium** Supported Moves vs input are ambiguous (Glossary *Move*, FR-1). Wide moves, slices and rotations are "supported where the curriculum or an Algorithm needs them", and many OLL Algorithms use `r`, `f` and `M`. FR-1's "any supported Move by gesture or by button" leaves UX unsure whether the Learner can *input* slice and wide moves (needed for *Guide me*/*Test me* on LL Lessons). *Fix:* state which Move types are input-able, and by which method.
- **low** Adjective bounds remain. FR-7 "clearly distinguishes"; NFR-2 "typical interaction"; FR-15 "large randomized corpus" (NFR-3 says "agreed corpus"); Gate 4 "documented usability issues addressed" (all? blocking-severity only?). *Fix:* pin numbers or severity rules, e.g. "corpus of 10,000 random states" and "all severity-1/2 issues addressed".
- **low** FR-3 redo is untested. Only undo and reset have consequences. *Fix:* "Redo after undo restores the undone state; any new Move clears the redo stack."

## Scope honesty — strong
§6.1 Non-Goals does real work: no smart cubes, no LLM correctness, no backend, no monetization. Each FR's Out-of-Scope block is explicit (FR-16, FR-18, §4.8). P1/P2/Not-planned are separated in §6.2. The schedule growth is surfaced honestly in a [NOTE FOR PM] rather than hidden. Open-items density is about 9 OQs + 18 inline assumptions + 1 NOTE. That is moderate for a green-light PRD, and most OQs are correctly deferred to architecture spikes (OQ-3/4/7/8).

### Findings
- **medium** Addendum roadmap compresses the riskiest camera work (addendum §F P0.5). Quick Scan, start-from-my-cube *and* Case recognition all land in week 12, a single week, and Quick Scan's "perspective correction is the hardest part" (OQ-7). P0.3 also packs the full Course, Physical-cube Mode, persistence, playback, 2-look LL and the Library into 3 weeks. The addendum is not a requirements source, but it is the input to OQ-9. *Fix:* re-estimate before OQ-9 is closed, or add a sentence to the PRD saying that the cut order is expected to fire.
- **low** Only one [NOTE FOR PM] (§6.2). The FR-27 gating question and the UJ-1/FR-9 tension are real unresolved tensions that deserved one. *Fix:* see the Done-ness findings.
- **low** NFR-11 30 MB may be tight (NFR-11, OQ-4, OQ-7). Kociemba pruning tables plus an optional TFLite model plus 3D assets is unverified. It is tagged [ASSUMPTION], which is acceptable. *Fix:* add a check of table size to the OQ-4 decision.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary is thorough, and §0 tells consumers where depth lives. FR IDs are global and contiguous (FR-1..FR-29: FR-21..28 camera, FR-29 P1 drills). Every feature names the UJs it realizes, and every UJ (1–6) is realized by at least one feature. UJ protagonists are named, with context carried inline (UJ-6 restates "Diego (UJ-2)"). Problems are mostly mechanical, but they are exactly what `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` extracts. Out-of-order UJ and SM IDs, a wrong section pointer, a stale "10 weeks", and assumptions that don't roundtrip will produce bad traceability.

### Findings
- **medium** Assumptions Index roundtrip is broken (§10). See Mechanical notes. Downstream skills use the Index to find what to validate.
- **medium** Stale "10 weeks" in OQ-5 (§9 OQ-5). "when Gate 4 runs within the 10 weeks" contradicts §6.2's ~14 weeks. *Fix:* change it to "within the P0 schedule (OQ-9)".
- **low** Terms used but not defined: "Stage Completion Predicate" (UJ-5, FR-27). The Glossary defines Completion Predicate per Objective, and Stages have a Mastery Condition. *Fix:* add "Stage Completion Predicate", or say "the Completion Predicate of each Stage's final Objective".
- **low** Addendum is stale on camera (addendum §A port map "observation (manual input now, camera later)"; §F epic sketch "E10 camera (P1)"). The second is footnoted; the first is not. *Fix:* change it to "manual input + camera (P0)".

## Shape fit — strong
This is a consumer product with meaningful UX that sits at the top of a UX → architecture → stories chain. UJs with named protagonists, a Glossary and testable FR consequences are the right shape. A dedicated Release and Portfolio section (§7) is a good fit for the dual purpose: launch plus portfolio. Rigor is appropriate for a definite Play Store release; the PRD is not over-formalized for a solo project. UJ-4 (hiring manager) is a legitimate stakeholder journey for the portfolio goal, though it is thin (see Substance).
